Match engine
------------

This is a simple match engine for a football game.

The football pitch is divided into 11 zones:

- The two 'goal boxes', representing the area in the 6 yard box
- nine zones (1-9) for the bottom left, bottom center, bottom right,
  middle left, top left etc.

The two teams are called Blue and Red. Blue defends goal area 0 and
Red goal area 10.

                    RedGoal
 --------------------------------------------
|          |    |     10     |    |          |
|    7     | 8  |____________|    |    9     |
|          |                      |          |
 --------------------------------------------
|          |                      |          |
|    4     |          5           |    6     |
|          |                      |          |
 --------------------------------------------
|          |     ____________     |          |
|    1     | 2  |            |    |    3     |
|          |    |     0      |    |          |
 --------------------------------------------
                   BlueGoal

The match state at a given point in time consists of the following
information:

- The minute of the match, between 1 and 90
- The red and blue players (including the zone each player is currently in)
- The ball (including the zone it is current in and which team/player has it)
- The match score

The zone containing the ball is called the 'active zone', and the
player with the ball is the 'active player'. The team with the ball is
the 'attacking team' and the team without the ball is the 'defending
team'.

The transition from one match state to another (a 'tick') is driven by
a football 'action' attempted by the player with the ball.

The actions included in the first version are:

- pass within zone (success or interception)
- pass forward zone (success or interception)
- pass lateral zone
- pass backward zone
- run to another zone (success or tackled)
- take a shot (goal, miss or save)

A roll is made to determine the outcome of the action, which can have
various levels of success, including turning the ball over to the
opponent. Various factors influence the success, including the number
of friendly and opponent players in the current zone and the skill of
those players.

The outcome of the action has various impacts on the game state:

- The score can change
- The minute of the match is advanced depending on how long the action
  took (a goal can be several minutes, a short pass a fraction of a minute)
- The active player can change
- The active zone can change
- The zone of any or all player on either team can change

Each player has a defined position or role, such as center back. The
players role has an impact on how they act during an action. A center
back is unlikely to move into an advanced zone during an attack.

An action example
-----------------

The ball is with a Blue CB in zone 2. The other Blue players in the
zone are the other CB, and an MC. The Red players are two of the
forwards. The other players are distributed as you might expect.

The options for an action in this situation are:

- A pass within of the zone to either the CB or MC (Z).
- A lateral pass to zones 1 or 3 (L/R)
- A forward pass to 5 (F)
- A backward pass to the goalkeeper in 0 (B)
- Run/dribble with the ball into zone 5 (D)
- Take a shot. (S)

Note that each action has a target zone.

The base chance ('attractiveness') of each action in this position is
scored 0-5.

- Z2:    2 (low because it doesn't advance the ball)
- D5:    4 (high because it advances the ball)
- L1/R3: 3 (medium)
- F5:    5 (high because it advances the ball)
- B0:    1 (low because doesn't advance the ball)
- S10:   1 (very low because we're a long way from the goal)

The first round of modification is to account for a comparison of the
players in each zone. In Zone 2, Blue outnumbers Red by one player, so
the attractiveness of action Z is increased by 1 (to 3).

- Z2: 2+1=3
- D5: 4-2=2 (because there are two more Red players in zone 5)
- L1: 3+0=3 etc.

This is the only modification round in the MVP, but other modification
rounds might be based on player attributes, team instructions and
gamemaster modifications.

Once all modification rounds are finished, the numbers are summed to
n, an n-teamd dice is rolled and the and the action is chosen.

Assume F5 (a forward pass into zone 5) is chosen. The possible
outcomes are a successful pass and an interception. There is first a
d20 "Execution" roll for pass quality. A 20 score (critical hit) is an
automatic success, a 1 is an automatic fail, and anything below 4 is a
miskick.

Assuming the execution check is passed but not critical we go to the
outcome generation phase: In zone 5 there are 4 players: 1 blue and 3
red. Count 2 points for every Blue player, and 1 for every Red. So 2
vs. 3. Roll a d5 and if the result is 1 or 2, the pass has succeeded,
but if it's 3,4 or 5, the ball is intercepted.

Assume it was a success. First, pick the player who now has the ball -
which in this case is the only blue player. The impact of this on the
match state must now be determined:

- the minutes counter will increase between 0.5 and 1.5 minutes.
- the ball will change zones and players
- Each player except the one who started the action with the ball and
  (if appropriate) the one who has the ball will 'react' to the action
  outcome, by rolling to determine if they move to an adjacent zone or
  not.

Things that aren't going to be included in the first version
------------------------------------------------------------

- Player stamina
- Player attributes
- Extra time
- Any set pieces
- Any actions apart from the ones listed, including no set pieces
- Kickoffs
- Tactics and instructions.
- Substitutions
- The global modifiers
- The game master
